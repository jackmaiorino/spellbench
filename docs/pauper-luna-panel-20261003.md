## Visibility and policy inputs

Luna receives the neutral observation and offered candidate semantics. The projection emits public battlefield, graveyard, exile and stack facts, the acting player's hand, and only current authorized looks, searches or reveals. Opponent unrevealed hands and libraries stay hidden. `known_cards:false` discloses the lack of a complete historical knowledge tracker. Viewer-local opaque IDs bind exact card incarnations; native object IDs, stack counters, RNG state, seeds and private clone requests are not included in Luna's prompt. A frozen public producer name may survive that producer leaving play, but does not reconnect to a new incarnation. Targeted trigger announcements and spells suspended during optional costs retain their exact stack identity.

`x_kernel_flat_v4` carries the pinned V4 model input tensor, numeric row bindings and contract digests through the trusted host channel. The host forwards decision extensions to trusted child adapters, including Luna's broker. The broker excludes all extensions before constructing model prompts; native IDs and tensors in this channel are not sent to the model. This is a model-prompt boundary, not isolation from the trusted child process. Ordinary decisions preserve their tensor and sampled-row mapping. Blockers, arrangements, selection ordering and trigger ordering use `deterministic-completion-logprob/v1`: each neutral candidate is completed by taking the first remaining legal choice, evaluated on private clones within that same decision group, and scored by the sum of native log probabilities. The adapter samples once per neutral decision with the pinned native categorical sampler. It does not expose a later opponent decision or advance authoritative state during scoring. These adapted policies have unchanged weights but are not claimed identical to their historical v1 behavior.

The declared scope is the eight compiled decklists, not every card in the engine catalog. Unsupported reachable choices halt visibly. Native V5 projects initiative but has no monarch field; no live native monarch projection is claimed, and monarch cards are absent from these decklists. Engine defaults retain automatic mana payment, combat damage assignment and replacement ordering. The trusted child processes are unsandboxed; isolation is self-reported. Private engine source and checkpoint files prevent independent public game replay, while published match ledgers permit rating recomputation. Qualification must establish actual completed-game throughput and budget feasibility before formal dispatch.

Private previews cache one exact validated prefix, bound to the same game and authoritative root step. Divergent prefixes restart from a fresh clone. Ordinary steps, resets, completed previews and refused prefixes discard the cache. Cached and fresh-clone replies must match exactly, and previews must leave the authoritative game, RNG and counters unchanged.

Completion proposals use lossless `integer-rle-table/v1` arrays and a zlib/base64 payload on the private policy channel. Identical arrays are stored once and resolved through exact table indices. The scorer also retains the earlier plain and `integer-rle/v1` representations. Float bit words remain unsigned 32-bit integers and metadata indices remain signed 64-bit integers. The native scorer requires exactly one payload representation and a complete checksum-verified zlib stream, with at most 64 MiB decompressed JSON, one million words per vector and 64 million expanded words per request. Each use of a table entry counts toward that aggregate scoring bound; missing, wrongly typed or nested references are rejected. The public wire limit remains 8 MiB. Plain, packed, compressed and table requests must produce identical logits and sampled choices. These bounds refuse oversized choices rather than approximating their policy score.
